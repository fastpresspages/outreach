# Deals without the cringe

The goal is not to "close" everyone. It is to turn a useful diagnosis into paid work for the few people who need the fix done for them.

Everything here is a starting point. Adjust for your market, currency, taxes and experience. Never quote a number you cannot deliver.

## The ladder

1. **Free automated scan.** The CLI and the checklist. No email required.
2. **Free human audit.** One specific email with the three fixes that matter most. No call required.
3. **Paid deep audit.** A written report, a call and a prioritized plan.
4. **Paid fix sprint.** You implement the highest-impact fixes.
5. **Paid build.** A new launch page or a rebuild, scoped in writing.

The free audit is the marketing. Do not turn it into a 45-minute discovery call.

## Three offers

| Offer | Who it is for | Deliverable | Starting point (USD) | Timeline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Deep audit** | A team launching in 2-6 weeks that wants a second pair of eyes | Recorded walkthrough, written report, prioritized fixes, one follow-up call | $150-400 | 3-5 business days |
| **Fix sprint** | A page with a clear conversion or trust problem | Implemented fixes, before/after LaunchGrade reports, handoff notes | $750-2,000 | 1-2 weeks |
| **Launch build** | Someone with no page yet, or a page that needs a rebuild | One launch page, form, legal pages, SEO/social metadata, A+ audit, handoff | $1,500-5,000 | 2-4 weeks |

Optional **care plan**: a monthly scan, a short report and up to two small fixes. Start around $100-300/month only if there is enough work to justify it. Do not sell a retainer for the sake of recurring revenue.

## Qualify before you quote

Ask these in one short reply:

1. What are you launching, and when?
2. Who is it for, and what should they do on the page?
3. What happens after someone submits the form?
4. What have you already tried?
5. Who makes the decision, and what budget range is realistic?

If the answers are vague, offer the free audit and stop. A proposal is not a discovery call.

## Disqualify early

- Wants guaranteed rankings, conversions or revenue.
- Wants fake testimonials, logos or user counts.
- "Just need a quick page" with no copy, no offer and no decision maker.
- Haggles before the scope is written.
- Needs it in 24 hours.
- Will not share the URL or the current numbers.
- Asks you to copy a competitor's page exactly.

Say no in one sentence and leave the door open: "This is not a fit for how I work, but the free scan will still be useful: [link]."

## Proposal template

Keep it under 250 words. No deck.

```text
Subject: [Project] — scope and next step

Hi [Name],

You said the goal is [outcome] before [date]. The page currently [specific problem]. The scan found [two or three concrete issues].

Here is what I propose:

Outcome: [what will be true at the end]
Scope: [3-5 bullets, what is included]
Not included: [copywriting beyond X, paid ads, ongoing work]
Timeline: [start date] to [end date]
Price: [amount], 50% to start, 50% on handoff
Revisions: two rounds within the agreed scope
Next step: reply "go" and I will send the invoice and a short brief.

The before/after will be measured with the same public LaunchGrade scan, so you can check the work.

[Name]
```

## Money mechanics

- **50% upfront** for new clients. Larger projects can use milestones, but never start unpaid.
- **Written scope.** A one-page proposal is enough. Include what is not included.
- **Two revision rounds.** Extra rounds are a new scope.
- **Invoice before delivery of the final files.** For a build, keep the site in your staging until the balance clears.
- **Use a payment method with a paper trail.** Wise, PayPal, Stripe or a bank transfer. Add taxes and fees to the quote, not as a surprise.
- **No equity for a launch page.** If you take equity, it is a separate investment decision, not payment for work.

## Follow-up without nagging

Three touches, then stop.

1. **Day 0:** the audit and the specific fix list.
2. **Day 3:** one useful addition, not "just checking in". Example: a screenshot of the fixed metadata or a competitor's public page.
3. **Day 10:** close the loop. "I am closing the file. The scan is still free if you want it later."

No fake deadlines, no "I have three other clients waiting", no guilt.

## When they ask for free work

"Happy to do the free audit. Implementation is paid work. If you want, I can send a fixed-price scope for the top three fixes."

Then send the scope. Do not do "a little extra" for free twice.

## Red flags

- They want a discount before they know the scope.
- They cannot name the decision maker.
- They want you to promise a number.
- They ask you to remove the privacy page because "it looks cluttered".
- They want to pay after "we see results".
- They treat the audit as a free consulting session.

## What to measure

- Audits sent
- Replies
- Calls booked
- Proposals sent
- Proposals won
- Average project value
- Repeat clients

Count these by hand in a spreadsheet. No tracking pixels, no CRM subscription, no invented pipeline.

## The honest close

The best sales line is the work itself:

> "Here are three things costing you signups. I can fix them this week for [price]. If you would rather do it yourself, the checklist is free."

That is the whole playbook.
