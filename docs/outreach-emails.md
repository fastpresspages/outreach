# Outreach emails that do not sound like outreach

**Rules before any send**

- You actually looked at the page. The specific observation is true and verifiable.
- One ask. One link. No deck, no calendar link in the first email.
- No fake compliment, no "I noticed you're losing customers", no fake deadline.
- Send from a real name and a real inbox. Reply like a human.
- If they say no, thank them and stop. One follow-up maximum.
- Never mass-send. Twenty personalized emails beat 2,000 merged ones.

## 1. Cold audit offer — founder with a public launch page

**Subject:** `A quick note on [page]'s launch page`

> Hi [Name],
>
> I ran my open-source launch checker on [URL] this morning. The page does the hard part well: [specific true thing, e.g. "the offer is clear in the first screen" or "the pricing section answers the obvious objection"].
>
> The scan found [one specific issue, e.g. "no Open Graph image, so shares look blank"] and [second issue]. I put the full report here: [report link]. It is free and takes about a minute to read.
>
> No pitch. If the report is useful, use it. If you want me to walk through the top three fixes, reply and I will send a short loom.
>
> — Meet
> [proof-of-work page]

**Do not send if:** you did not run the scan, the report is not public, or the "specific thing" is generic.

## 2. One follow-up, four days later

**Subject:** `Re: A quick note on [page]'s launch page`

> Hi [Name] — one follow-up, then I will leave you alone.
>
> The highest-impact fix from the report is [fix], because [one sentence reason]. If it helps, here is the exact change: [link or two-line instruction].
>
> If now is not the time, no problem. The report will stay at the same URL.
>
> — Meet

**Do not:** add "just bumping this", guilt, a new pitch or a fake deadline.

## 3. Collaboration — adjacent open-source maintainer

**Subject:** `Idea: a joint check for [their tool]`

> Hi [Name],
>
> I maintain [LaunchGrade], a zero-dependency CLI that audits launch pages. [Their project] solves [specific adjacent problem], and I think our users overlap.
>
> Concrete idea: I add one check that detects [their thing] correctly, with a fixture and a credit to you. You get a free, permanent audit path; I get a check I would have written badly alone. No money, no exclusivity, no logo swap.
>
> If that is useful, I will open a draft PR this week. If not, I will still point people to [their project] when it is the right answer.
>
> — Meet

**Do not:** ask them to promote you in the first email. Lead with the artifact.

## 4. Sponsorship or partnership

**Subject:** `Sponsoring the free launch audit (one deliverable, no logo wall)`

> Hi [Name],
>
> FastPress Labs gives away a launch-page audit CLI, a GitHub Action and an MCP server. The tools are free and the reports are public.
>
> I am looking for one sponsor for [specific thing, e.g. "the next 20 human audits"] — not a logo wall. The deliverable is [a named report, a workshop, a maintained check], published with a date and a source. You get a short "sponsored by" line and the raw results, including the unflattering ones.
>
> If that is interesting, I will send a one-page scope with a price and a timeline. If not, no follow-up.
>
> — Meet

**Do not:** invent audience numbers. If you do not have them, say "no audience numbers yet; here is the work."

## 5. Podcast or newsletter pitch

**Subject:** `Pitch: what 10 launch-page audits taught me about trust`

> Hi [Name],
>
> I run LaunchGrade, a free CLI that grades launch pages. I recently audited [number] public pages and found a pattern: [specific, surprising, true finding, e.g. "the pages with the best design often had the weakest trust signals"].
>
> A 20-minute segment could cover: the three checks founders ignore, the one fix that is almost always worth it, and why "AI-ready" is mostly about being readable without JavaScript.
>
> I can bring the before/after reports and a live audit of one page. No fee expected; I am happy to be a useful guest, not a sponsor read.
>
> — Meet

**Do not:** pitch a topic you have not actually researched.

## 6. Paid build proposal — after a real conversation

**Subject:** `Scope for [project] — three fixes, two weeks`

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for the call. Here is the smallest version that solves the problem we discussed.
>
> **Outcome:** [one sentence, measurable]
> **Deliverables:** [three bullets, concrete]
> **Not included:** [one or two things, explicitly]
> **Timeline:** [start date] to [end date], with one review point.
> **Price:** [fixed price or range]. 50% to start, 50% on delivery. No retainer.
>
> If this looks right, reply "approved" and I will send a one-page agreement. If the scope is wrong, tell me which line and I will revise once.
>
> — Meet

**Do not:** send this before you understand the problem. Do not hide the price until a call if the buyer asked for it.

## 7. After an audit — quote request

**Subject:** `Was the audit useful?`

> Hi [Name],
>
> Glad the report was useful. If you have a minute, I would like to quote one specific sentence from you on the FastPress Labs site — for example, what changed after the audit. I will show you the exact quote and link before it goes live, and you can decline.
>
> Either way, here is the final report: [link].
>
> — Meet

**Do not:** write the quote for them and ask them to approve it. That is fabrication with extra steps.

## 8. Community partner — workshop or joint clinic

**Subject:** `A free launch-audit clinic for [community]`

> Hi [Name],
>
> I run FastPress Labs, a small open-source project around launch-page audits. I would like to run a free 45-minute clinic for [community]: two volunteer pages, live scans, three fixes each, recording for the community.
>
> I will handle the format and the write-up. You get a useful session and a permanent resource; I get practice and a few honest conversations. No email list capture, no pitch.
>
> Would a [month] slot work?
>
> — Meet

**Do not:** use the community's members as a lead list. No signup form, no follow-up sequence.

## Follow-up cadence

| Day | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Send one personalized email. |
| 4 | One follow-up with a new useful detail. |
| 10 | Close the loop: "I will assume now is not the time." |
| After | Never again unless they reply. |

## What a good reply rate looks like

There is no universal benchmark, and anyone who gives you one is guessing. Track **your** sends, replies, audits and calls. If 20 genuine sends produce zero replies, the problem is the offer, the audience or the observation — not the follow-up cadence.
