### What counts as a completed audit

A real page, a real report, a human reviewed the top fixes, and the owner received it. A CLI run with no follow-up does not count. Neither does a "would you like an audit?" email.

### What counts as a collaboration conversation

A reply that discusses a specific joint artifact: a guest check, a template, a workshop, a co-published teardown, a sponsorship with deliverables. "Let's connect" does not count.

### What counts as a paid pilot

A written scope, a price, a start date and a deliverable. A verbal "maybe" does not count. A discount for a case study is fine only if the case study is real and approved.

## Non-goals

- 10,000 GitHub stars. Stars are a lagging indicator, not a strategy.
- Going viral. One useful thread is enough.
- A newsletter before there is something worth sending.
- Paid ads before organic proof.
- A "community" with no reason to return.
- Any metric that requires a tracking pixel on a page that promises no analytics.

## Weekly review

Answer these in five lines, with links:

1. **What shipped?** Link the commit, page or report.
2. **Who replied?** Names/companies only if they opted in; otherwise "2 replies, both declined".
3. **What did we learn?** One sentence, not a mood.
4. **What is the next smallest useful thing?** One action, due date.
5. **Did we publish anything we cannot source?** If yes, fix it before the next send.

## Day 90 decision

If there is no paid pilot by day 90, do **not** invent demand. Interview five people who used the free audit and did not buy. Ask what they did instead. Then choose:

- **Narrow the offer** to the one fix people already ask for.
- **Keep it free** and pursue sponsorship or collaborations.
- **Stop** the paid track and keep the tool as a public good.

All three are honest outcomes. Only fake traction is not.
